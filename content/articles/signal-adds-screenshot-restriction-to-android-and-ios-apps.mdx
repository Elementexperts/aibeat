<h2>What happened</h2>
<p>Signal announced that it will limit users’ ability to take screenshots within its secure messaging app, introducing a new restriction for both Android and iOS platforms.</p>
<p>The move builds on Signal’s earlier approach of blocking screenshots by default in its Windows desktop application.</p>
<p>Although the setting can be disabled by users, turning it off would break the privacy guarantees that Signal promises its users.</p>
<p>AI safety researcher Meredith Whittaker has described screen‑scanning AI agents as an existential threat to Signal’s privacy model, noting that such agents can capture, store and make sensitive data accessible to local or remote databases.</p>

<h2>AIBeat analysis</h2>
<p>This could lead users to weigh the convenience of capturing messages against the risk of exposing private conversations, especially if they choose to disable the restriction; the concern is amplified by expert warnings that AI agents might automatically harvest screen content, potentially undermining the confidentiality that Signal aims to provide.</p>
