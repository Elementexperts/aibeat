<h2>Background</h2><p>Google has announced that it will now allow users to remove visible watermarks from its AI-generated content. This move has significant implications for developers, founders, and researchers who work with AI tools.</p><h3>What happened</h3><p>Previously, Google's AI-generated content, such as images and videos, featured a visible watermark that identified the content as AI-generated. This watermark was intended to provide transparency and help users identify AI-generated content. However, it also limited the use of this content in certain applications.</p><h2>Why it matters</h2><p>The ability to remove visible watermarks from AI-generated content matters because it raises questions about ownership and authenticity. Without a visible watermark, it may be more difficult to determine whether a piece of content is AI-generated or not. This could have significant implications for industries such as media, advertising, and education, where the use of AI-generated content is becoming increasingly common.</p><h3>Impact on developers and founders</h3><p>Developers and founders who work with AI tools need to be aware of the implications of removable watermarks on AI-generated content. They should consider the following:</p><ul><li><strong>Authenticity</strong>: How will you verify the authenticity of AI-generated content that does not feature a visible watermark?</li><li><strong>Ownership</strong>: Who owns AI-generated content that does not feature a visible watermark?</li><li><strong>Transparency</strong>: How will you provide transparency about the use of AI-generated content in your applications?</li></ul><h2>What to do about it</h2><p>Developers and founders who work with AI tools should take the following steps:</p><ul><li>Review your AI-generated content policies and procedures to ensure that they are up-to-date and address the issue of removable watermarks.</li><li>Consider implementing additional measures to verify the authenticity of AI-generated content, such as digital fingerprints or other forms of metadata.</li><li>Provide transparency about the use of AI-generated content in your applications, including disclosures about the use of AI-generated content and the potential risks and benefits associated with it.</li></ul><h3>Invisible benchmarks</h3><p>It's worth noting that Google's invisible benchmarks, which are used to identify AI-generated content, will still be in place even if the visible watermark is removed. These benchmarks are designed to provide an additional layer of transparency and authenticity, and they will continue to play an important role in identifying AI-generated content.</p><table><thead><tr><th>Feature</th><th>Visible Watermark</th><th>Invisible Benchmark</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Transparency</td><td>Provides visible indication of AI-generated content</td><td>Provides invisible indication of AI-generated content</td></tr><tr><td>Authenticity</td><td>Helps to verify the authenticity of AI-generated content</td><td>Helps to verify the authenticity of AI-generated content</td></tr><tr><td>Ownership</td><td>Does not affect ownership of AI-generated content</td><td>Does not affect ownership of AI-generated content</td></tr></tbody></table>
