<h2>What happened</h2>
<p>Meta paused the Muse sharing feature after security researchers reported that public conversation links could be easily discovered.</p>
<p>Researchers at Claroc found that Muse web addresses for shared conversations were generated from the conversation title and were written into public DNS, making them searchable.</p>
<p>Meta’s David Singleton said the underlying issue with Muse artifact URLs is now fixed and sharing is back on; artifacts shared before the fix now have new URLs, and old links display a note directing users to obtain the new link from the sharer.</p>

<h2>AIBeat analysis</h2>
<p>This could mean that users who previously received a Muse link need to request an updated URL from the original sharer to continue accessing the conversation.</p>
<p>The fix reduces the risk that anyone could locate a shared conversation by searching public DNS, suggesting improved privacy for users of Meta’s AI chat product.</p>
