<h2>What happened</h2>
<p>Meta introduced Muse Gadgets, an open‑source project that lets developers build hardware that connects to Muse, and provided open‑source firmware and a Linux SDK for the platform.</p>
<p>Suggested project ideas include a color e‑ink display or a stick that plugs into a TV’s HDMI port, and developers can use hobbyist computers such as Raspberry Pi or ESP32 boards to attach displays, buttons, sensors and actuators.</p>
<p>Meta also created a Discord channel to support Muse Gadgets users.</p>
<p>Nat Friedman announced Muse Home Link, a USB‑C‑powered device that lets Muse connect to a home network and smart devices; Meta produced 5,000 units and is giving them away for free to Muse subscribers while supplies last, with shipping expected in a few weeks.</p>
<p>Meta introduced Muse for Small Business, a free offering with usage limits that connects Muse to Shopify, Dropbox and Slack, and launched a new Meta Enterprise Platform business unit to promote its AI services to corporate customers.</p>

<h2>AIBeat analysis</h2>
<p>These moves could enable hobbyist developers to prototype custom Muse accessories quickly, expanding the assistant’s reach into homes and workplaces; the free Home Link devices lower the barrier for networked integration, while the small‑business tier gives merchants a ready‑made way to embed Muse into existing workflows.</p>
