<h2>What happened</h2>
<p>OpenAI announced ChatGPT Wallet, a wallet for its Dots agents.</p>
<p>The wallet is rolling out today for ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the United States who are at least 18 years old and have eligible US‑issued American Express, Chase, Mastercard or Visa cards.</p>
<p>After adding a card, Dots agents can use the wallet to buy items in response to user prompts, but they must check with the user each time before checkout, and every purchase requires a biometric security check such as Face ID or a fingerprint scan.</p>
<p>OpenAI replaces the credit‑card details with a payment token, so the actual card information is not shared with the Dots agent or the merchant.</p>
<p>OpenAI expects users will eventually be able to delegate purchases to agents by setting spending limits, purchase categories, or time periods and pre‑approving them.</p>
<p>Some retailers may block AI agents; earlier this year Amazon blocked Meta’s Muse agents, and websites need to ensure agents are not inadvertently blocked while payment systems must recognize legitimate purchases made by agents.</p>

<h2>AIBeat analysis</h2>
<p>This could let Pro users make purchases through conversational prompts while keeping card data private, because tokenization and biometric verification add security; however, retailer blocking may limit where agents can complete transactions, suggesting users need to verify merchant compatibility before relying on the wallet.</p>
