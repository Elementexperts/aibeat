<h2>Funding round and roadmap</h2>
<p>Relativity Networks announced a $22 million Series A led by <strong>Andreessen Horowitz</strong> with participation from <strong>DCVC</strong> and <strong>Lightspeed Venture Partners</strong>. The capital will fund the scaling of its proprietary hollow‑core fiber (HCF) manufacturing line, pilot deployments in Tier‑1 data‑center hubs, and the hiring of a sales engineering team focused on enterprise cloud providers.</p>
<p>Founded in 2023, the company claims its HCF can transmit data up to <strong>30% faster</strong> than conventional silica‑based fiber while consuming less power. The Series A brings total funding to $32 million, positioning Relativity to move from lab‑scale prototypes to commercial roll‑outs in 2027.</p>
<h2>Why hollow‑core fiber matters now</h2>
<p>Modern workloads—large language model training, real‑time analytics, high‑frequency trading—are hitting the limits of traditional networking. Latency budgets measured in microseconds translate directly to revenue or research throughput. Hollow‑core fiber replaces the glass core with a low‑index‑air channel, allowing light to travel at a higher fraction of the speed of light and reducing signal dispersion.</p>

<p>These gains are most pronounced over distances typical of inter‑rack or intra‑pod connections—10 km to 100 km—making HCF a natural fit for hyperscale data‑center campuses and emerging edge clusters.</p>
<h2>Implications for developers and founders</h2>
<p>For developers, the immediate benefit is lower end‑to‑end latency without having to rewrite application logic. Distributed training pipelines that currently spend 15‑20% of wall‑clock time on data shuffling could see that fraction cut to under 10%, accelerating model iteration cycles.</p>
<p>Founders building SaaS platforms that rely on real‑time data streams—gaming, video collaboration, IoT telemetry—should reassess their network topology. If a data‑center provider adopts Relativity’s HCF, moving latency‑sensitive services onto that infrastructure can provide a competitive edge without additional code changes.</p>
<p>Investors and CTOs should also note the cost angle. The reduced power draw per terabit translates into lower OPEX for large‑scale operators. Early adopters may negotiate favorable pricing in exchange for pilot traffic, creating a win‑win for both the fiber supplier and the tenant.</p>

<ul>
