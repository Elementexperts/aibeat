<p>An automation can prepare a useful result without being allowed to act on it immediately. Splitting those stages makes a small-business pilot easier to inspect.</p><h2>Show the proposed action</h2><p>For an email, show the recipient and full message. For a record change, show the old and proposed values. For a publication, show the final page and destination. A reviewer should see the action they are approving, not just a vague completion notice.</p><p>Set clear rules for exceptions. Missing information, conflicting instructions or an unexpected destination should pause the workflow and go to its owner.</p><h2>Plan for mistakes</h2><p>Record who approved an action and its result. Decide how to stop the workflow, correct a bad update and avoid repeating an action after a retry. Begin with drafts and reversible changes before considering broader permissions.</p><p>Approval is only useful when the reviewer has enough context and time to make a decision. Measure that workload as part of the pilot, rather than treating it as free.</p><p><em>AIBeat practical guidance. For broader background, see the voluntary <a href="https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework">NIST AI Risk Management Framework</a>. This checklist is not a NIST certification or a prescribed implementation.</em></p>
